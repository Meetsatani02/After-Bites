### 1. "Dubai Chocolate" — Fix Dessert Chocolatier

| | |
|---|---|
| **Campaign / Brand** | The *Can't Get Knafeh Of It* bar / Fix Dessert Chocolatier |
| **Country** | United Arab Emirates |
| **Launch year** | Product ~2022; global explosion 2024, still live into 2026 |
| **Duration** | Rolling / ongoing (pop-ups worldwide through 2026) |

- **Brand background:** Tiny home-grown Dubai maker founded 2021 by Sarah Hamouda; recipe born from pregnancy cravings. Not a big-budget brand — the entire phenomenon was organic.
- **What the brand wanted:** Sell a premium, culturally-rooted chocolate bar at scale from a single city.
- **Consumer insight:** A dessert can become a *watchable object* — "hard enough to brag about, easy enough to copy."
- **Core idea:** Fuse a traditional Middle-Eastern sweet (knafeh + pistachio + tahini) into a modern chocolate bar with a signature sensory "reveal."
- **Execution:** Sold only via Deliveroo, dropping at 5pm daily in Dubai and selling out in minutes (~AED 385 for six flavours). Deliberate scarcity.
- **How it went viral:** ASMR "snap-and-pull" videos revealing the molten green pistachio centre hit hundreds of millions of views. The texture *was* the ad.
- **How UGC was generated:** (a) "is it worth the hype" taste-tests; (b) DIY copycat recipe videos — home-made "Dubai chocolate" became its own content genre.
- **How consumers participated:** Unboxings, reaction videos, and recreating the bar at home.
- **Instagram / social role:** Entirely TikTok/Reels-native, driven by ordinary creators, not paid influencers.
- **Influencers/creators:** Organic creator pickup only.
- **PR / earned media:** Global press; supermarket and Lindt copycats; credited with contributing to a **global pistachio-cream shortage** in 2024–25 — an extraordinary earned-media hook.
- **Results:** Hundreds of millions of views; a worldwide copycat category; supply-chain impact.
- **Awards:** None — a purely organic phenomenon.
- **Why it worked:** The product itself performed a repeatable, filmable "moment."
- **What made it memorable:** The green-ooze snap became a visual signature people could recognise instantly.
- **✅ What After Bites can learn:** Engineer a *signature sensory moment* into the product experience (the tin's ASMR open, the colourful mukhwas, the crunch/refresh) so filming it is irresistible. Use controlled scarcity for launch drops.
- **⛔ What NOT to copy:** Don't rely on permanent artificial scarcity — it frustrates a habit/ritual product that needs to be *always available* when the craving hits.
- **Adaptation:** An **"#AfterBiteASMR"** format — lid-pop, pour, the refreshing crunch — plus limited festive "drop" tins for hype.

## PART B — STRATEGIC ANALYSIS: THE COMMON PATTERNS

Answering your Section 9 questions directly, from the evidence above.

**What makes people voluntarily create UGC?**
Three ingredients recur: (1) a **repeatable, low-effort format** they can copy in seconds (Grimace, Dubai chocolate snap, Swiggy waveforms); (2) an **identity or status payoff** — the post says something about *them* (Kachra Awards = "I'm savvy," CHAGEE merch = "I have taste"); (3) a **built-in prop** — the product or a tool does the visual work, so they don't have to be creative. People don't share brands; they share *themselves using* a brand that flatters them.

**What makes people share a product with friends?**
A sensory surprise or a "you have to try this" moment (Pulse's tangy centre, Dubai chocolate), or genuine social utility. Mukhwas has a unique, under-exploited advantage here: **it is one of the few products people physically offer to everyone around them.** Sharing is already the native behaviour — the campaign just has to name and celebrate it.

**What makes a campaign culturally relevant?**
Speed and participation, not budget (Amul at 0.4% of spend; Aldi in 24 hours). Relevance comes from *reacting to* culture and *handing people a role in it*, not broadcasting at them.

**How does a brand become associated with a category?**
By dramatising the association publicly and repeatedly until it's reflexive (Heinz). The brand must (a) attach itself to a specific *moment/situation* (after a meal, in the car, after a wedding feast), and (b) get the public to keep re-proving the link.

**How does a brand turn a product into a ritual?**
Name the gesture, stage it to be filmed, and tie it to a fixed trigger (CHAGEE's brewing; the after-meal moment). A ritual = a fixed cue → a fixed action → a small reward, repeated. "After a meal → the After Bite → fresh + shared."

**How can After Bites make Paan Mukhwas culturally exciting for younger consumers?**
Reframe from "digestion/old-fashioned" to **confidence, freshness, generosity and travel-cool** — heritage with modern design (the CHAGEE + Bombay Sweet Shop move). Ride the clean-label / tobacco-free tailwind (FoodPharmer movement) as a badge of the new generation.

**Road trips / travel essential / carry-everywhere:**
Own a *situation*. Make the tin a **glovebox/handbag/desk object** by design (pocket format, car-friendly, spill-proof), then seed the "no highway meal is complete without it" and "pass it around the car" behaviours through micro-creators until it's a norm.

**How to create a campaign customers join without being paid?**
Give them a tiny, fun, status-earning action with a shot at recognition (Wakefit's open call, Kachra's certificate, Swiggy's waveforms). Zero-cost to enter, high emotional payoff, renewable.

**How can customers become the marketing channel?**
Turn the product's *natural* shared moment into a named, film-friendly format with a hashtag and light rewards — so every tin passed around a car or wedding table is a potential post.

**How do people come to say "Paan Mukhwas means After Bites"?**
Relentless association of the brand with the **specific after-meal/travel moment**, a **named ritual**, a **repeatable UGC format**, and eventually a **Heinz-style "prove the association" mechanic** — sustained over a festive-calendar rhythm, not a single burst.

**The through-line:** the strongest, cheapest campaigns make the **product itself the media**, give people a **role rather than a message**, and **repeat on a rhythm** until the brand owns a moment.

## PART C — ORIGINAL CAMPAIGN CONCEPT FOR AFTER BITES

> Built for **Gen Z + young millennials**, **organic-first**. Nothing here copies a single campaign above — it *composes* their proven principles around one true, ownable insight about mukhwas.

### The core insight
**Mukhwas is the most generous thing on the table.** It's one of the only foods people instinctively *offer to everyone* — passed hand-to-hand in the car, around the dinner table, at the office desk, at a wedding. The sharing is already happening. No one has *named* it or *owned* it.

### Campaign name: **"Aage Badhao"** (Pass It On) — *After Bites. The taste worth sharing.*

### The Big Idea
Turn the natural act of **passing the tin around** into a named, film-friendly cultural ritual — *the After Bite* — and a signature Reel format. Every time an After Bites tin travels from one hand to the next (car → desk → dinner → wedding), it's a branded moment. The product's own social behaviour becomes the media.

### Brand connection
The name literally means *after eating*. "Aage Badhao" fuses the two things After Bites owns: the **after-meal freshness moment** and the **generosity of sharing it**. The tin, passed on, physically enacts word-of-mouth.

### Why people will participate
- It flatters them (generous, fun, culturally-fluent).
- It's a **2-second, zero-skill format** — pass the tin, take a pinch, refresh.
- It works in every setting they already film (road trips, hostels, offices, weddings).
- Light recognition/rewards + collectible tins.

### UGC mechanism — **The "Pass" Reel**
A **transition Reel** (a proven, easy viral format): the After Bites tin passes from person to person, each takes a pinch and freshens, the tin swipes across the lens as the scene cuts to a new place/person. Chain it across contexts for the "epic pass." Branded audio + an AR "pass" filter make it copy-paste easy.
- **Layer 2 — #AfterBiteASMR:** the lid-pop, the pour, the crunch/refresh (borrowing the Dubai-chocolate sensory-reveal principle).
- **Layer 3 (later) — "Say Paan, Think ___":** the Heinz-style association game, once the brand has reach.

### Hashtag strategy

### How it goes viral
A trivially-copyable format + a hashtag + the product's *own* built-in sharing behaviour + micro-creator seeding + collectible/status payoff. The audience becomes the media because the product is already being passed — the campaign just names, stages, and rewards it.

### How it builds long-term brand memory
A **named ritual** ("the After Bite" / "Aage Badhao") attached to a **fixed trigger** (after every meal, every journey), repeated on a **festive-calendar rhythm**, with **collectible packaging** — the CHAGEE + Heinz + Bombay Sweet Shop compounding effect.

### How it builds "Paan Mukhwas = After Bites"
Every pass associates the brand with the exact moment (after-meal / travel / gathering). Sustained, and later crowned with a Heinz-style "Say Paan, Think ___" mechanic, the brand becomes the reflexive answer — not because it said so, but because customers kept passing it on.
